# Topic 4: Word Order, Questions, and Negation

## Lesson 4.1: Basic Word Order and Questions

### Theory

In a simple German main clause, the usual pattern is:

`Subject + Verb + ...`

The conjugated verb usually stands in position 2.

Example:

- `Ich wohne in Berlin.`

### Yes/No questions

Formula:

`Verb + Subject + ... ?`

Examples:

- `Wohnst du in Berlin?`
- `Hast du ein Auto?`

### W-questions

| German | English |
|---|---|
| wer | who |
| was | what |
| wo | where |
| wohin | where to |
| woher | where from |
| wann | when |
| wie | how |
| warum | why |

Formula:

`Question word + verb + subject + ... ?`

Example:

- `Wo wohnst du?`

### Common mistakes

1. Putting the verb too late
2. Using English word order for German questions
3. Forgetting the verb-second rule

## Lesson 4.2: Negation with `nicht` and `kein`

### Theory

German has two very common ways to make a sentence negative.

### `nicht`

Use `nicht` to negate:

- verbs
- adjectives
- adverbs
- an entire clause

Examples:

- `Ich komme nicht.`
- `Das ist nicht teuer.`

### `kein`

Use `kein` to negate:

- nouns without an indefinite article
- nouns when you want to say "not any" or "no"

Examples:

- `Ich habe kein Auto.`
- `Er hat keine Zeit.`

### Formula

`Subject + verb + nicht`

- `Ich schlafe nicht.`

`Subject + verb + kein/keine + noun`

- `Ich habe kein Handy.`

### Common mistakes

1. Using `nicht` before a noun instead of `kein`
2. Putting `nicht` in the wrong position
3. Saying `nicht Auto` instead of `kein Auto`
