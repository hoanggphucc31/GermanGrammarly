# Topic 3: Present Tense Verbs and Separable Verbs

## Lesson 3.1: Regular and Common Irregular Verbs in Present Tense

### Theory

Most German verbs appear in dictionaries in the infinitive form:

- `machen`
- `lernen`
- `kommen`

To form the present tense, remove `-en` and add the correct ending.

### Regular verb endings

| Pronoun | Ending | Example: lernen |
|---|---|---|
| ich | -e | lerne |
| du | -st | lernst |
| er/sie/es | -t | lernt |
| wir | -en | lernen |
| ihr | -t | lernt |
| sie/Sie | -en | lernen |

### Formula

`Subject + conjugated verb + ...`

Example:

- `Ich lerne Deutsch.`

### Common irregular pattern

Some common verbs change their vowel in the `du` and `er/sie/es` forms.

Examples:

- `fahren -> du faehrst, er faehrt`
- `lesen -> du liest, er liest`
- `sprechen -> du sprichst, er spricht`

### Common mistakes

1. Using the infinitive after the subject
2. Forgetting the `-st` ending with `du`
3. Forgetting the vowel change in common irregular verbs

## Lesson 3.2: Separable Verbs

### Theory

Some German verbs have a prefix that separates in a normal present-tense sentence.

Examples:

- `aufstehen` = get up
- `einkaufen` = shop
- `anrufen` = call
- `mitkommen` = come along

### Rule

In a normal statement:

- the conjugated verb stays in position 2
- the prefix moves to the end of the clause

### Formula

`Subject + verb stem (conjugated) + ... + prefix`

Example:

- `Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.`

### Common mistakes

1. Not separating the prefix
2. Putting the prefix in the wrong place
3. Forgetting that the conjugated verb still stays in position 2
