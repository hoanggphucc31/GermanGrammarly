# Topic 1: Nouns, Articles, Gender, and Plural

## Lesson 1.1: Nouns and Articles in German

### Theory

In German, every noun should be learned with:

1. Its grammatical gender
2. Its article
3. Its singular and plural form

German has three genders:

| Gender | Definite Article | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | der | the |
| Feminine | die | the |
| Neuter | das | the |

### Indefinite articles

| Gender | Indefinite Article | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | ein | a/an |
| Feminine | eine | a/an |
| Neuter | ein | a/an |

### Structure formula

`Article + Noun`

Examples:

- `der Mann`
- `die Frau`
- `das Kind`

### When to use

Use articles when:

- introducing a noun
- talking about a specific person or thing
- learning new vocabulary

### Important rule

Always learn the noun as a full phrase, not by itself.

Good:

- `der Tisch`

Not recommended:

- `Tisch`

### Common mistakes

1. Learning nouns without the article
2. Mixing up `der / die / das`
3. Using `eine` with a neuter noun
4. Thinking natural gender is always the same as grammatical gender

Example:

- `das Maedchen` = the girl, but grammatically it is neuter

## Lesson 1.2: Plural Forms

### Theory

German plural forms are not made in only one way. Some common patterns are:

- `-e`
- `-en`
- `-er`
- `-n`
- no ending change
- sometimes an umlaut appears: `a -> ae`, `o -> oe`, `u -> ue`

### Article in plural

All plural nouns use the definite article:

- `die`

German does not have a true indefinite plural article like English `some`.

### Structure formula

`Singular noun -> Plural noun`

Examples:

- `der Tisch -> die Tische`
- `die Frau -> die Frauen`
- `das Kind -> die Kinder`

### When to use plural

Use plural when talking about:

- more than one person
- more than one object
- groups in general

### Common mistakes

1. Using the wrong article with plural nouns
2. Adding `-s` to every noun
3. Forgetting that the plural definite article is always `die`
