# Module 12 — Exam Tips: Positionsverben & Genitiv

## Goethe Zertifikat B1 Strategy

### 1. Schreiben (Writing Part 2 & 3)

* **Using Genitiv to sound professional:** In formal writing, simple possessive Genitiv already lifts your language clearly to B1.
    * *das Auto des Vaters*
    * *die Meinung der Eltern*
    * *die Nutzung des Internets*
* **Proper-name Genitiv is a fast win:**
    * *Marias Tasche*
    * *Davids Auto*
    * *Thomas' Wohnung*

### 2. Lesen (Reading Part 2 & 5)

* **Recognizing Genitiv fast:** In reading, watch for `des/der` and the extra `-s/-es` on masculine/neuter nouns.
    * *das Ende des Films*
    * *die Farbe des Autos*
* **Spoken alternative awareness:** `von + Dativ` can often express the same relationship in everyday German.
    * *das Auto vom Vater* = *das Auto des Vaters*

### 3. Sprechen (Speaking Part 1 & 2)

* **Describing pictures or slides (Part 2):** When describing what is on a slide or giving an example, use the state verbs correctly.
    * *Auf dem Bild **steht** ein Mann.* (Not "stellt"!)
    * *Die Flasche **liegt** auf dem Boden.* (Not "legt"!)
* **Organizing an event (Part 1):** When deciding where to put things, use the action verbs.
    * *Wir können den Tisch in die Mitte **stellen**.*
    * *Ich **hänge** das Plakat an die Tür.*

### 4. 📚 Expert Addition: Genitiv prepositions

* `wegen/trotz/während/statt + Genitiv` are very useful for B1 reading and writing.
* But remember: they are an extension beyond the tight core of `Kap. 63`, which mainly teaches possessive Genitiv.

### 5. Grammar Trap Reminder: The "Wo" vs "Wohin" test
If you are unsure whether to use Dativ or Akkusativ with *in, an, auf, unter*, etc., ask yourself:
* Is something moving from A to B? -> Action -> **Wohin? -> Akkusativ**
* Is something staying in one place? -> State -> **Wo? -> Dativ**
