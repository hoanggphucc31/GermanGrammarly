## Part 1: Positions- und Richtungsverben (Kapitel 62)

In German, there is a strict difference between **a state** (being somewhere) and **an action** (moving/putting something somewhere). 
We use two different verbs and two different cases for this.

### 1.1 The Golden Rule: Wo? vs. Wohin?
* **Wo? (Where?)** → Describes a state / location. Requires **Dativ**. Uses **strong/irregular verbs**.
* **Wohin? (Where to?)** → Describes an action / movement. Requires **Akkusativ**. Uses **weak/regular verbs**.

### 1.2 The 4 Verb Pairs (SiSe, SteSte, LiLe, HäHä)

| Wo? (State) + Dativ | Wohin? (Action) + Akkusativ |
|---|---|
| **sitzen** (to sit / be sitting) | **setzen** (to set / seat someone/oneself) |
| **stehen** (to stand / be standing)| **stellen** (to place upright) |
| **liegen** (to lie / be lying) | **legen** (to lay / place flat) |
| **hängen** (to hang / be hanging) | **hängen** (to hang something up) |

> *Notice: "hängen" is the same word for both! But its past tense forms are different.*

### 1.3 Examples in Action

**1. Sitzen vs. Setzen**
* *Das Kind **sitzt** auf dem Stuhl.* (Wo? auf dem Stuhl = Dativ. The child is sitting.)
* *Die Mutter **setzt** das Kind auf den Stuhl.* (Wohin? auf den Stuhl = Akkusativ. The mother seats the child.)

**2. Stehen vs. Stellen**
* *Die Flasche **steht** auf dem Tisch.* (The bottle is standing on the table.)
* *Ich **stelle** die Flasche auf den Tisch.* (I put the bottle upright on the table.)

**3. Liegen vs. Legen**
* *Das Buch **liegt** auf dem Bett.* (The book is lying flat on the bed.)
* *Ich **lege** das Buch auf das Bett.* (I lay the book flat onto the bed.)

**4. Hängen vs. Hängen**
* *Das Bild **hängt** an der Wand.* (The picture is hanging on the wall.)
* *Ich **hänge** das Bild an die Wand.* (I hang the picture onto the wall.)

### 1.4 The Past Tense Forms

| Verb | Präteritum | Perfekt |
|---|---|---|
| **sitzen** (State) | saß | hat gesessen |
| **setzen** (Action)| setzte | hat gesetzt |
| **stehen** (State) | stand | hat gestanden |
| **stellen** (Action)| stellte | hat gestellt |
| **liegen** (State) | lag | hat gelegen |
| **legen** (Action) | legte | hat gelegt |
| **hängen** (State) | hing | hat gehangen |
| **hängen** (Action)| hängte | hat gehängt |

## Part 2: Der Genitiv (Kapitel 63)

You already know Nominativ, Akkusativ, and Dativ. Welcome to the 4th and final case: **Der Genitiv** (Possessive case).

### 2.1 What is it for?
The Genitiv shows possession. It means "of the" or acts like the English "'s".
* *Das Auto **des Vaters**.* (The car of the father / The father's car.)
* *Das Spielzeug **des Kindes**.* (The toy of the child.)

### 2.2 How to form the Genitiv

Only Maskulin and Neutrum nouns get an extra "-s" or "-es" at the end of the noun! Feminin and Plural only change the article.

| Gender | Article | Noun Ending | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maskulin** | **des** / eines | **-s** or **-es** | Das Auto **des Vater-s** |
| **Neutrum** | **des** / eines | **-s** or **-es** | Das Buch **des Kind-es** |
| **Feminin** | **der** / einer | (no change) | Die Tasche **der Mutter** |
| **Plural** | **der** / - | (no change) | Die Schule **der Kinder** |

> **Rule for -s vs -es:** 
> Short nouns (1 syllable) usually take **-es**: des Kindes, des Mannes, des Jahres.
> Longer nouns take **-s**: des Lehrers, des Vaters.

### 2.3 Genitiv with Proper Names (Eigennamen)

In German, you can use a proper name in the Genitiv just like English's "'s".
The name comes **BEFORE** the noun it possesses, and gets an **-s** (no apostrophe!).

* *Das ist **Davids** Auto.* (That is David's car.)
* *Das ist **Annas** Tasche.* (That is Anna's bag.)
* *Wir besuchen **Marias** Familie.* (We are visiting Maria's family.)

> ⚠️ If the name ends in -s, -ß, -x, or -z, you add an **apostrophe** instead:
> * *Das ist **Thomas'** Wohnung.* (That is Thomas's apartment.)
> * *Das ist **Max'** Idee.* (That is Max's idea.)

### 2.4 The Spoken Alternative: von + Dativ

In everyday spoken German, many people avoid the Genitiv entirely and use **von + Dativ** instead.

| Written / Formal (Genitiv) | Spoken / Informal (von + Dativ) |
|---|---|
| *Das Auto **des Vaters*** | *Das Auto **von meinem Vater*** |
| *Die Tasche **der Lehrerin*** | *Die Tasche **von der Lehrerin*** |
| *Das Spielzeug **des Kindes*** | *Das Spielzeug **von dem Kind*** |

> 🎯 **Expert Tip for B1 Exam:** In the **Schreiben** (Writing) section, always use the Genitiv! It shows you can handle formal German. In the **Sprechen** (Speaking) section, "von + Dativ" is perfectly acceptable.

## 3 · 📚 Expert Addition: Prepositions with Genitiv

> ⚠️ **Note:** These 4 prepositions are essential for B1, but they are **not the core focus** of Kap. 63 (*Das Auto seines Vaters*), which centers on possessive Genitiv. We include them here as a pedagogical supplement because they are closely related and frequently tested.

At B1, you must know these 4 prepositions. After them, the noun MUST be in Genitiv!

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **wegen** | because of | **Wegen des Regens** bleibe ich zu Hause. |
| **trotz** | despite / in spite of | **Trotz des Wetters** gehe ich spazieren. |
| **während** | during | **Während des Urlaubs** habe ich viel gelesen. |
| **statt** | instead of | **Statt eines Autos** kaufe ich ein Fahrrad. |

> *(Note: In spoken street German, many people use Dativ after "wegen" and "trotz" (z.B. wegen dem Regen). But in B1 writing, you MUST use Genitiv!)*

## 4 · 📚 Expert Addition: The Complete Case Map

Now that you know all 4 cases, here is the master table for the definite articles (der, die, das, die):

| Case | Maskulin (der) | Feminin (die) | Neutrum (das) | Plural (die) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nominativ** | der | die | das | die | Subject (Der Mann) |
| **Akkusativ** | **den** | die | das | die | Direct Object (Ich sehe den Mann) |
| **Dativ** | **dem** | **der** | **dem** | **den + n** | Indirect Obj / Location (Ich helfe dem Mann) |
| **Genitiv** | **des + s/es** | **der** | **des + s/es** | **der** | Possession (Das Auto des Mannes) |
