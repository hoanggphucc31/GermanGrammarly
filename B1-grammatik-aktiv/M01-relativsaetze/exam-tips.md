# Module 01 — Exam Tips: Relativsätze

> 🎯 **Goethe B1:** Relative clauses are tested heavily in **Lesen** (reading comprehension — you must recognize them!) and **Schreiben** (writing — using them makes your text more sophisticated).

---

## How This Grammar Appears

| Section | What to Watch |
|---|---|
| **Lesen** | Long sentences with embedded relative clauses — you must identify which noun is being described. |
| **Schreiben** | Using relative clauses earns bonus points for complexity: "Ich habe einen Freund, **der** in Berlin wohnt." |
| **Sprechen** | Describing people/places: "Das ist der Lehrer, **bei dem** ich Deutsch gelernt habe." |
| **Hören** | Descriptions with relative clauses in interviews and reports. |

## Key Tips

1. **The Comma Signal**
   * In a B1 reading text, when you see a comma + der/die/das/den/dem, it's almost certainly a relative clause.
   * Everything from that pronoun until the verb at the END is a relative clause.

2. **The 3-Step Decision**
   * **Step 1:** What noun is being described? → Gender/Number
   * **Step 2:** What role does it play in the relative clause? → Nom/Akk/Dat
   * **Step 3:** Combine → Relativpronomen

3. **Schreiben Strategy**
   * Instead of writing two short sentences, combine them:
   * ❌ *Ich habe eine Freundin. Sie kommt aus der Türkei.*
   * ✅ *Ich habe eine Freundin, **die** aus der Türkei kommt.*

4. **The "denen" Trap**
   * Dativ Plural is the ONLY form that differs from articles.
   * *Die Leute, **denen** ich geholfen habe...* (NOT den!)

> 🇻🇳 *Mẹo: Trong bài thi Lesen, khi gặp câu dài, hãy tìm dấu phẩy + der/die/das — đó là bắt đầu mệnh đề quan hệ. Bỏ qua mệnh đề đó để tìm ý chính của câu trước, rồi quay lại đọc chi tiết.*
