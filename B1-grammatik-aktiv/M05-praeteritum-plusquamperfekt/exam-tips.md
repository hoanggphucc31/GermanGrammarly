# Module 05 — Exam Tips: Präteritum & Plusquamperfekt

## Goethe Zertifikat B1 Strategy

### 1. Schreiben (Writing Part 1 & 2)

* **Use Präteritum for structure:** In the B1 writing tasks, you often need to describe an experience. While Perfekt is fine, throwing in a strong Präteritum verb makes your text look more advanced.
    * Good: *Ich bin nach Berlin gefahren. Es war schön.*
    * Better: *Letztes Jahr **fuhr** ich nach Berlin. Das Wetter **war** herrlich.*
* **The "nachdem" trick for extra points:** Examiners look for complex sentences. Using `nachdem` + Plusquamperfekt is a guaranteed way to show B1 mastery.
    * Try to fit one into your text: *Nachdem ich den Artikel gelesen **hatte**, wollte ich Ihnen schreiben.* (After I had read the article, I wanted to write to you.)

### 2. Lesen (Reading)

* **Recognize Präteritum quickly:** The reading texts in the B1 exam (especially the news articles and informative texts) use Präteritum extensively. You must recognize forms like `wurde`, `kam`, `fanden`, `gab` instantly without having to translate them in your head.
* **Plusquamperfekt = Cause:** If you see `hatte...ge-`, it usually explains the cause or background of the main event in the text.
    * *Die Polizei fand den Dieb schnell. Er **hatte** seinen Ausweis am Tatort **verloren**.* (The police found the thief quickly. He had lost his ID at the scene.)

### 3. Hören (Listening)

* In the radio reports or interviews, speakers often mix Perfekt and Präteritum. Don't let the vowel changes in Präteritum (like *bleiben* -> *blieb*, *gehen* -> *ging*) confuse you.

### 4. Sprechen (Speaking)

* **Stick to Perfekt mostly:** In spoken German, Perfekt is the standard. Don't try to use Präteritum for action verbs when speaking; it sounds unnatural and bookish.
* **The Big 3 Exceptions:** ONLY use Präteritum in speaking for `sein` (war), `haben` (hatte), and modal verbs (musste, konnte, wollte, sollte, durfte).
