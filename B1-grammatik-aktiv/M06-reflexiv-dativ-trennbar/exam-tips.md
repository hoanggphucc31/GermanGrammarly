# Module 06 — Exam Tips: Reflexive Dativ & Dual Verbs

## Goethe Zertifikat B1 Strategy

### 1. Sprechen (Speaking) & Schreiben (Writing)

* **Reflexive Dativ shines in B1!**
    * Using a reflexive Dativ verb correctly in your speaking or writing exam is a massive green flag for examiners. It shows you've moved past basic A2 grammar.
    * **Magic Phrase for Sprechen:** *Ich kann **mir** gut vorstellen, dass...* (I can well imagine that...). Use this when discussing an opinion or reacting to your partner's idea!
    * **Magic Phrase for Schreiben:** *Ich mache **mir** Sorgen, weil...* (I am worried because...). Great for personal emails/letters.

### 2. Lesen (Reading)

* **Spotting Dual Verbs in context:** Reading texts often feature inseparable verbs like *übersetzen* (translate), *unterschreiben* (sign), and *wiederholen* (repeat). 
    * Remember that these DO NOT have "ge-" in the Perfekt. If you see *Er hat den Vertrag unterschrieben*, don't get confused looking for a "ge-". It is the past participle.

### 3. Hören (Listening)

* **Stress tells the story:** In the listening exam, pay attention to word stress. 
    * If the speaker stresses the prefix (*Wir steigen **UM***), it's separable (literal meaning).
    * If they stress the main verb (*Bitte wieder**HOL**en Sie*), it's inseparable (figurative meaning).

### 4. General B1 Pitfall

* **Don't over-correct yourself.** Many B1 students learn about Dativ reflexive and suddenly start using *mir/dir* everywhere. 
* Only use it if there is a DIRECT OBJECT in the sentence, or if it's a fixed expression like *sich etwas vorstellen*.
    * *Ich freue **mich**.* (Correct)
    * ~~Ich freue mir.~~ (WRONG!)
