# Module 13 — Exam Tips: N-Deklination & Adjektive als Nomen

## Goethe Zertifikat B1 Strategy

### 1. Schreiben (Writing Part 1 & 2)

* **The "Student" Trap:** In the B1 exam, you might write about studying or asking someone for help. If you write *Ich frage der Student* or *Ich frage den Student*, the examiner immediately knows you don't master N-Deklination. 
    * ✅ *Ich frage den Student**en**.*
    * ✅ *Ich fahre mit einem Kolleg**en**.*
* **Using abstract concepts:** Using *etwas* or *nichts* with an adjective noun makes your writing sound very natural and advanced.
    * *Gibt es in deiner neuen Stadt **etwas Interessantes**?* (Is there anything interesting in your new city?)
    * *Leider habe ich **nichts Neues** zu berichten.* (Unfortunately I have nothing new to report.)

### 2. Sprechen (Speaking Part 2 - Presentation)

* **Addressing people:** When talking about society or rules, you will often use adjectives as nouns. Make sure to get the endings right!
    * *Viele **Jugendliche** verbringen zu viel Zeit am Handy.* (Many teenagers spend too much time on their phones.)
    * *Für **Erwachsene** ist das oft schwer zu verstehen.* (For adults, this is often hard to understand.)
* **The "Deutsche" Trap:** If you introduce a friend or talk about an experience, do not say *ein Deutsche* for a man!
    * ❌ *Mein Freund ist ein Deutsche.*
    * ✅ *Mein Freund ist ein Deutsch**er**.*

### 3. Lesen (Reading)

* **Don't get confused by the "-en":** When you see a word like *den Namen* or *dem Präsidenten* in a reading text, recognize that this is singular, NOT plural. The "-en" is just the N-Deklination marker for Akkusativ/Dativ/Genitiv.
    * *Er spricht mit dem Journalisten.* = He is talking to THE journalist (Singular).

### 4. Grammar Summary

* **N-Deklination (der Junge, der Kollege):** Base word is a NOUN. Add -(e)n in Akk/Dat/Gen.
* **Adjektive als Nomen (der Deutsche):** Base word is an ADJECTIVE. Capitalize it, and apply normal adjective endings (-e, -en, -er, -es) depending on the article before it.
