# Module 13: N-Deklination & Adjektive als Nomen

> **CEFR Goal:** Can correctly decline nouns belonging to the n-declension group, and can use adjectives as nouns (e.g., "der Deutsche", "etwas Neues") with correct endings.

## Part 1: Die N-Deklination (Kapitel 64)

### 1.1 What is the N-Deklination?
In German, a specific group of nouns (mostly **Maskulin**) gets an extra **-n** or **-en** ending in **ALL cases except Nominativ singular**.

| Case | Normal Noun (der Mann) | N-Deklination (der Student) |
|---|---|---|
| **Nominativ** | der Mann | der Student |
| **Akkusativ** | den Mann | den Student**en** |
| **Dativ** | dem Mann | dem Student**en** |
| **Genitiv** | des Mann**es** | des Student**en** |

*(Notice how "der Student" becomes "den/dem/des Studenten"!)*

### 1.2 Which nouns belong to this group?

Almost all of them are **Maskulin** and refer to **people or animals**. There are three main groups to memorize:

**Group 1: Maskulin nouns ending in "-e" (People/Animals)**
* *der Junge* (the boy) -> den Jungen
* *der Kollege* (the colleague) -> den Kollegen
* *der Kunde* (the customer) -> den Kunden
* *der Affe* (the monkey), *der Löwe* (the lion)

**Group 2: Nationalities ending in "-e"**
* *der Türke* (the Turk) -> den Türken
* *der Franzose* (the Frenchman) -> den Franzosen
* *der Russe* (the Russian) -> den Russen

**Group 3: Academic/Professional titles from Latin/Greek (-ent, -ant, -ist, -at)**
* *der Student* -> den Studenten
* *der Patient* -> den Patienten
* *der Polizist* -> den Polizisten
* *der Automat* (Exception: Not a person! The machine) -> den Automaten

**The Exceptions (Must Learn!):**
* **der Herr** (the gentleman) -> den Herr**n** (Only adds -n, not -en)
* **der Mensch** (the human) -> den Mensch**en**
* **der Name** (the name) -> den Namen -> Genitiv: des Name**ns** (Adds an extra 's'!)

## Part 2: Adjektive als Nomen (Kapitel 65)

### 2.1 Turning an Adjective into a Noun
You can turn an adjective into a noun by **capitalizing** it. 
* *deutsch* (German) -> *der Deutsche* (the German man) / *die Deutsche* (the German woman).
* *krank* (sick) -> *der Kranke* (the sick man).

### 2.2 The Trick: They still decline like adjectives!
Even though they are capitalized like nouns, they still take **adjective endings** depending on the article before them.

| Context | Maskulin (The German man) | Feminin (The German woman) |
|---|---|---|
| **Definite Article** (der/die) | **der** Deutsche | **die** Deutsche |
| *(Akkusativ)* | Ich kenne **den** Deutsch**en**. | Ich kenne **die** Deutsch**e**. |
| **Indefinite Article** (ein/eine)| **ein** Deutsch**er** | **eine** Deutsch**e** |
| *(Akkusativ)* | Ich kenne **einen** Deutsch**en**.| Ich kenne **eine** Deutsch**e**.|
| **No Article** (Plural) | - Deutsch**e** | - Deutsch**e** |

> ⚠️ **Common Mistake:** Many students say *Ich bin ein Deutsche* (if male). WRONG! The adjective ending for "ein" (maskulin) is "-er". It must be: *Ich bin ein Deutsch**er**.*

### 2.3 Other Important Adjective-Nouns
* *der Angestellte / ein Angestellter* (the employee)
* *der Bekannte / ein Bekannter* (the acquaintance)
* *der Erwachsene / ein Erwachsener* (the adult)
* *der Verwandte / ein Verwandter* (the relative)
* *der Jugendliche / ein Jugendlicher* (the youth/teenager)

### 2.4 Abstract Concepts: etwas / nichts + Adjektiv
If you want to say "something new" or "nothing special", you use **etwas** or **nichts** + **capitalized adjective ending in -es**.

* *Ich habe **etwas Neues** gelernt.* (I learned something new.)
* *Das ist **nichts Besonderes**.* (That is nothing special.)
* *Wir brauchen **etwas Wichtiges**.* (We need something important.)
* *Gibt es **etwas Gutes** im TV?* (Is there something good on TV?)

## 3 · 📚 Expert Addition: N-Deklination vs. Adjektive als Nomen

These two topics are often confused because they both involve extra "-en" endings. Here is how they differ:

| Feature | N-Deklination (der Student) | Adjektive als Nomen (der Deutsche) |
|---|---|---|
| **Does the base word change?** | No, "Student" is a pure noun. | Yes, it comes from an adjective (deutsch). |
| **Is there a female form?** | Yes, but it's a new word: **die Studentin**. | Yes, the exact same word, just different article: **die Deutsche**. |
| **"ein" form (Nominativ)?** | ein Student (No ending) | ein Deutsch**er** (Takes adjective ending!) |
| **"den" form (Akkusativ)?** | den Student**en** | den Deutsch**en** |
